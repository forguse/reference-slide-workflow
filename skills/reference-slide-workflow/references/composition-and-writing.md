# Composition, alignment and writing

## Preserve style, adapt the argument

Choose the composition from the new page's job. A comparison benefits from shared axes or rows. A mechanism benefits from directional links. Evidence needs a readable figure and a nearby interpretation. A cover and closing page need a different hierarchy from a dense content page. Do not infer “everything must be a three-card grid” from one reference page.

Extract a spacing scale from the reference. For a 1280-wide page, 16–24-unit insets and 20–32-unit column gaps can be starting estimates only when no measurements exist; scale to the actual canvas and typography. Dense references can use smaller spacing with a clear hierarchy. Keep a small number of spacing values instead of arbitrary per-object offsets.

## Optical alignment

Match visible figure content to visible text, not merely the image rectangle or a text baseline. Inspect internal whitespace and axis labels before cropping. Text above/beside a figure should begin on the intended common edge. In the same column, headings, body and conclusions share their alignment anchor. A centered module heading can remain centered within that module; it need not share the page title's left edge.

Use whole-block vertical centering for multiline table cells. Give captions and sources their own space. Keep connecting rules in a separate band, or break the line behind a label with an actual opaque plate. Do not allow a line to run through glyphs. Verify gradients at the weakest contrast point, especially white text at a pale end stop.

Automated geometry uses actual SVG coordinate transforms and declared relationships. It cannot recognize meaningful content inside a picture. Explicit optical offsets are acceptable when their cause is recorded. The remedy for a failed alignment check is a design fix, not a larger global tolerance.

## Typography and density

Match the reference's font character, size ratios, weights and line spacing. A research topic does not imply a serif font. A sans-serif reference should not become a serif deck merely to look formal. Conversely, a deliberately editorial serif reference should retain that character if the font is available.

For expressive typography, use [typography and image relationships](typography-and-image.md). Font count is determined by the reference and page job. Maintain recognizable roles across the deck while allowing different pages to use different combinations, orientations and image relationships. Intentional editorial offsets are local design choices, not alignment failures or a reason to disable unrelated checks.

Do not confuse SVG units with PowerPoint points. Keep text editable and preserve the converter's text model. Shorten, restructure or split overloaded content before reducing type. Do not leave a tiny figure surrounded by empty white space when the reference's visual center is a large evidence image.

## Natural slide language

Use a specific subject title or a supported conclusion, whichever suits the page. Avoid vague labels, repeated slogans and repetitive defensive explanations. Qualify a claim where the qualification matters; use notes for supporting detail.

Do not default to leading middle dots or strings of terms separated by middle dots. A few ordinary list items are fine when they genuinely help scanning. Prefer explicit relationships: “模型预测与实测结果” is clearer than “模型 · 数据 · 验证” when the page actually compares prediction with measurement.

Separate style placeholders from factual evidence. Example numbers, photos and charts must not become the user's real results. Preserve units, axes, legends, sources and the comparison being made. Never crop evidence merely to make two pictures identical in size.
